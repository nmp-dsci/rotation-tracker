Flagged up front

Four things the corpus says about the design

Each one comes from a read-only pass over rt.transcript_chapters today (macro-flows and untagged sections only, the same 199 weeks the viewer shows), not from assumption.

Rotation is a third of the story. The taxonomy has to be wider or most of the commentary is lost decided 28 Oct: extract every commentary on price

By weeks mentioned: pullback or correction 150, market top or bottom 147, breakout 141, rotation 113, overvalued or expensive 106, hedging 89, undervalued or cheap 82, flows 78. If the extractor only looks for rotation it skips the valuation calls ("oil is still actually quite cheap versus gold") and the technical calls ("utility stocks are oversold, consumer staples are oversold") that are the other things you asked to measure. So the plan defines seven plays, with rotation first, and every mention carries the same fields so they can be measured the same way.

He almost never says "I'm buying". Measure the claim, not a trade

"I'm buying" or "I bought" appears in 3 weeks; "I'm selling" in none. What he says is "I would" (175 weeks), "I like" (90), "stay away" (7), "watch list" (5), or he reports what Wall Street is doing: options flow (160 weeks), sentiment (127), dark pool prints (94), retail (91), hedge funds (25). So a mention records whose view it is (his, or reported positioning), whether it is observed, expected or recommended, and how hedged ("a little bit of rotation out of semis"). The performance branch can then score each class separately instead of pretending every sentence was a trade.

His "industries" are trader sectors, not GICS. The asset map needs two layers

He talks in semis, software, Mag 7, small caps, regional banks, homebuilders, defensives, metals: themes that cut across the 11 GICS sectors. He also names the ETFs he watches (SPY in 38 weeks, IBIT 25, GDX 22, XLF 18, XLE 15, XLV 14, TLT 14, IWM 13, GLD 10, SMH 7, KRE 7, ITB 7, IGV 5). So the map has a theme layer with an ETF proxy each (semis → SMH, software → IGV, small caps → IWM, regional banks → KRE) and a GICS layer for single names (the S&P 500 list carries sector and sub-industry per name). A stock mention resolves to both: Nvidia is Information Technology / Semiconductors and also in the "semis" theme.

The repo is public and the extractor is an LLM. Two guards from day one

Everything the extractor writes contains transcript text (the quote), and the gold-label set is transcript text with answers attached. Both live in Postgres (rt.mentions, rt.labels) and .rt/, never in git; the repo holds the taxonomy, the universe (built from public lists), the prompt, and tests on made-up sentences. Second: a quote the model returns must be found verbatim in the section text or the mention is dropped and counted, so a hallucinated sentence can never become a row. Every mention keeps its extractor_version, prompt hash and model, and a full re-run costs about $17, so the taxonomy can change without fear.

assets/v1

The asset map: what a mention can be about, and the series that will judge it

A mention resolves to one row of rt.assets. Every row has a kind, a layer, aliases (how he says it, including the auto-caption's mishearings), and a proxy: the ticker whose price history will score the mention in the next branch. A theme without a clean proxy is still a row; its proxy is marked none rather than invented.

US equities, the definition
S&P 500: the 503 constituents of S&P Dow Jones Indices' large-cap index (500 companies, three with two share classes), taken from the public constituent list on the build date, with GICS sector and sub-industry per name. Today's list has all 11 sectors and 126 of the 163 GICS sub-industries. The same page's changes table gives membership dates back to 2023, so a name is tagged with the dates it was in the index (valid_from/valid_to); without that, 2023 mentions of names that joined later would be scored as index members they were not.
Nasdaq-100: the 100 largest non-financial Nasdaq-listed companies, from Nasdaq's own list at build time (about 20 of them are not in the S&P 500, mostly foreign listings). Union of the two lists ≈ 525 single names.
Everything else he names (Robinhood before it joined the index, MicroStrategy, SMCI, TSMC, Palantir early on) is added by hand to assets/extras.yaml as it turns up in the unknown-asset queue, with the same fields. Russell 2000 is a theme with proxy IWM only; its 2,000 constituents are not loaded.
GICS, the industry definition
The Global Industry Classification Standard (S&P and MSCI, 2023 revision): 11 sectors → 25 industry groups → 74 industries → 163 sub-industries. Single names carry sector and sub-industry; "industry" in your question maps to sub-industry (Semiconductors, Application Software, Regional Banks, Homebuilding) when he means a GICS group, and to a theme row when he means a trader basket.
Themes, the trader definition
The 18 keyword themes the viewer's chart already uses (themes/v0) become rows here with proxies and aliases, extended with the ones the corpus adds: regional banks (KRE), homebuilders (ITB), biotech (XBI), miners (GDX), defensives (a basket: XLP, XLU, XLV), AI (no single proxy; none, measured through its members).

Resolution is code, not the model: the extractor returns the asset as spoken plus its best ticker guess; mining/resolve.py maps that through rt.aliases to an id, and anything unmatched lands in a review queue (rt status reports the unknown rate). The crude name search above shows why a plain lookup won't do: "Nasdaq" matched Nasdaq Inc. in 164 weeks and "Target" the retailer in 78, when both are almost always the index and the word. Auto-captions add their own aliases: "Brett's" is breadth, "Marcus" is markets, "bookcase" is bull case, "the cues" is the Qs. These go in the alias table as they are found, with a test each.

Measuring the miner

How we will know the extractor is right before anything is measured against prices

The gold set

12 weeks, three per year, chosen at random from the 199 and fixed. Each gets a careful pass at high effort to propose mentions, then you spot-check 50 of them in the viewer (a thumbs up/down and a corrected field where needed) and every rotation mention in those weeks is read twice. Expected size: about 400 mentions. Labels live in rt.labels and .rt/gold/; the repo never sees them.

The metrics, per play and overall

  • Span precision and recall: a predicted mention matches a gold one if their quotes overlap and the play agrees.
  • Direction accuracy and asset accuracy on matched mentions; unknown-asset rate on all.
  • Agreement between two runs of the same version on the same weeks (the model is not deterministic; the number says how much).
  • v1 against v0: the regex baseline on the same gold set, so the LLM's contribution is a number, not a feeling.

Acceptance to run the backfill

Rotation precision ≥ 0.85 and recall ≥ 0.80; asset accuracy ≥ 0.90 on resolved mentions; unknown-asset rate < 10%; dropped-quote rate < 3%. Anything under that is a prompt or alias fix and another eval, at a few cents a week.

Tests in the repo (public, synthetic)

  • The resolver: made-up sentences for every alias and every ambiguous name ("Target hit its price target" resolves to nothing; "Target, the retailer, is oversold" resolves to TGT).
  • The locator: a synthetic section with known segments; a quote maps to the right span and timestamp; a quote that is not there is dropped and counted.
  • The schema: the extractor's JSON output is validated field by field; an unknown play or direction fails loudly.
  • The seed universe build: a fixture copy of ten constituent rows produces the expected rt.assets rows with sector, sub-industry and validity dates.
  • No live LLM call in CI. The one recorded fixture is a synthetic week and a synthetic response.
