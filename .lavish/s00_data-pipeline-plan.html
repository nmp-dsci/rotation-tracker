Four things that change the plan

Each one was checked against the live channel, your keys or the sibling repos today, not assumed.

The stock data you expected in nmp-central-ai isn't usable for this

The central Postgres holds market data in one place: DataAgentBench's benchmark snapshot in database dab. That's 2,754 per-ticker tables, including the sector ETFs, but every series ends on 1 April 2020 and nothing refreshes it. The only current daily prices in the portfolio are in value-invest-agent's DuckDB: 102 tickers to 18 Sep 2026, with SPY the only ETF. No repo has a ticker-to-sector mapping. So this plan adds a price loader to rotation-tracker's own database, copied from value-invest-agent's yfinance code.

The best pick isn't a weekday. It's the last upload before Monday's open

He is evidently in Australia: descriptions carry AU/NZ broker offers, and uploads land in the New York evening, which is Sydney's next morning. So each video recaps the US session that just closed. Once a week, usually Saturday or Sunday evening New York time, there's one more: chapters like "Market week overview" and "Upcoming earnings schedule" mark it as the weekly wrap plus a look ahead. From 2020 into 2023 it was mostly the "Buy Now, Wait or Sell The Stock Market This Week?" series.

2023 onwards costs about 199 credits

transcript·lab charges 1 credit per video when it's handed the metadata. That's what --metadata-json does, and value-invest-agent already uses it. 2023 onwards is 199 weekly picks, about 44% of the 452 credits, leaving ~253. A pilot of 8 videos measures the real cost per video before the backfill commits.

transcript·lab has side effects: reuse it the way value-invest-agent does

Don't use bulk-index --since, which costs about 3 credits a video. Don't use its HTTP ingest endpoints either, which cost 2. Run index-rag <url> --metadata-json as a subprocess from its folder. Each run also logs to transcript·lab's own MLflow file store, loads an embedding model and rewrites dashboard/rag_pipeline.html in that repo. Read transcripts back from its Chroma store read-only. This repo never writes there.

Proposed pick rule, pick/v1

For each trading week, keyed by its Friday F: take the last upload on the Videos tab (no Shorts, no live streams) published at or after that week's Monday 09:30 ET and before the next Monday 09:30 ET. Tag it weekend if it came after F 16:00 ET, otherwise fallback. A week with no upload gets no pick and a log line. Times are converted with America/New_York, so daylight-saving changes are handled. The tag goes in the table, so the later analysis can treat a fallback pick (a Thursday or Friday recap) differently from a true wrap.

The pipeline

Six commands, one database

Transcripts come from Supadata and nothing else. Step ④ is the only Supadata call (/v1/transcript, 1 credit), made by transcript·lab, which already holds your three keys. yt-dlp is used only for the free steps: the video list, exact publish times and chapters. It never fetches a transcript. Prices come from yfinance. Only step ④ costs anything. Everything lands in a rotation database on the central Postgres (schema rt), the M3 pattern every portfolio project follows.

Focus: macro and flows, not chart reading

A 2024+ weekly wrap has 13–19 chapters. Most are macro or flows, and 2–4 are chart levels, options levels or sponsors. Older videos lean more technical. Real chapter titles from the videos checked for this plan:

  • macro-flows Market week overview · Sector rotation and flows · Institutional vs retail flows · Sector shifts and earnings · Defensive rotation strategy · Bond market and debt risks · Yields, dollar, and small caps
  • technicals Technical chart analysis · Market index levels · S&P 500 levels and options · Trading levels for top stocks · Tech and semiconductor charts
  • promo / other Tiger Brokers · Pause Screen · Closing and live event

The next branch reads only macro-flows sections plus untagged videos. That means less noise and fewer model tokens. The tagger's version is stored with each tag, so it can be improved without re-fetching anything.

What this unlocks

Your SaaS question, the day the pipeline lands

Before any model runs, plain SQL over the synced transcripts and chapters answers the first half: when did he first say it? Prices answer the second half: what happened next? M2 replaces the keyword search with structured rotation calls (out → in, stated driver, quote, timestamp) resolved to rt.instruments.

1 · First mention (macro and flows sections only)

WITH q AS (SELECT websearch_to_tsquery('english',
  'software OR SaaS rotation OR selling') AS q)
SELECT p.week_friday, v.title, c.title AS chapter,
       ts_headline('english', c.text, q.q) AS said
FROM rt.transcript_chapters c
JOIN rt.weekly_picks p USING (video_id)
JOIN rt.videos v USING (video_id), q
WHERE c.topic IN ('macro-flows', 'untagged')
  AND to_tsvector('english', c.text) @@ q.q
ORDER BY p.week_friday
LIMIT 5;

2 · What happened next (relative to SPY)

-- sketch: IGV vs SPY from the first-mention week
SELECT h.months,
       a.adj_close / a0.adj_close
     - s.adj_close / s0.adj_close AS igv_minus_spy
FROM (VALUES (1),(3),(6),(12)) h(months)
CROSS JOIN LATERAL (...) a0  -- IGV at T0
CROSS JOIN LATERAL (...) a   -- IGV at T0 + h
CROSS JOIN LATERAL (...) s0  -- SPY at T0
CROSS JOIN LATERAL (...) s;  -- SPY at T0 + h

T0 is the pick's publish time. A weekend video becomes tradable at Monday's open. Point-in-time rules from AGENTS.md apply from day one.
